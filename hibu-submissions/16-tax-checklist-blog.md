## H1

Small business tax preparation checklist

## Opening (first paragraph — keep at the top)

To prepare a small business return you need last year's return, your year-end profit and loss statement and balance sheet, bank and credit card statements for the full year, payroll reports, records of any assets bought or sold, loan statements, and documentation for owner draws and distributions. Everything else follows from those.

## Before you start

Two things make this go quickly. First, your books should be closed for the year — not approximately, actually reconciled. Second, gather everything before the appointment rather than during it, because a return assembled in pieces takes longer and costs more.

If your books are not closed, start there instead.

## The checklist

**Business identity and history**
- Last year's business return, federal and state
- EIN letter
- Entity documents: articles of organization or incorporation, operating agreement
- S corporation election, Form 2553, if one was made
- Any notices received from the IRS or the Franchise Tax Board during the year

**Income**
- Year-end profit and loss statement
- Year-end balance sheet
- All 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC forms received
- 1099-K from payment processors
- Merchant processor year-end summaries
- Records of any income not reported on a 1099, including cash

**Expenses**
- Bank and credit card statements for all twelve months, every business account
- Receipts for large purchases
- Rent or lease agreements
- Insurance premiums
- Professional fees
- Vehicle mileage log, with business and total miles
- Home office details, if applicable: square footage of the office and of the home

**Payroll and contractors**
- Year-end payroll reports from your provider
- Forms W-2 and W-3
- Forms 1099-NEC issued to contractors
- Forms W-9 collected from contractors

**Assets**
- Anything purchased over your capitalization threshold, with date and cost
- Anything sold or disposed of during the year
- Prior-year depreciation schedule

**Debt and owner activity**
- Loan statements showing the year-end balance and interest paid
- Owner draws and distributions
- Owner contributions into the business
- Any personal expenses that ran through the business account

**Health, retirement and other**
- Health insurance premiums, including self-employed health insurance
- Retirement plan contributions
- Estimated tax payments made during the year, with dates and amounts

## The five people always forget

1. **Estimated tax payments.** They are payments you already made and they reduce what you owe. Missing them is the most expensive omission on this list.
2. **The 1099-K.** Payment processors issue them, they go to the IRS whether or not you noticed, and the reported figure is usually gross before fees and refunds.
3. **Owner draws mixed into expenses.** Very common, and it distorts both the return and the books.
4. **The mileage log.** Reconstructing it in April is painful. The IRS expects contemporaneous records.
5. **W-9s from contractors.** You need them before you can issue a 1099, and chasing them in January is a bad week.

## What the IRS actually expects you to keep

Your records need to show gross income, deductions and credits. For most small businesses the business bank account is the backbone, supported by receipts and invoices. Electronic records carry exactly the same requirements as paper ones. IRS Publication 583 sets out what a new or small business is expected to maintain, and it is short enough to read in one sitting.

*Link the words "IRS Publication 583" to https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p583.pdf*

## A note on deadlines

Business return deadlines come earlier in the year than the personal deadline, and they vary by entity type. Confirm your dates with your preparer for the year you are filing.

## FAQ

**What documents do I need for my small business taxes?**
Last year's return, year-end financial statements, twelve months of bank and card statements, payroll reports, asset purchases and sales, loan statements, and records of owner draws and distributions.

**Do I need receipts for everything?**
Bank and card statements establish what was spent. Receipts matter most for large purchases, assets and anything that would be questioned. Publication 583 sets out the expectation.

**What if my books are not done?**
Close them first. A return prepared from unreconciled books is a return that gets amended.

**Do I need to send 1099s before I file?**
Yes, and the contractor deadline falls earlier than most business return deadlines. Collect W-9s well before year end.

**Can I deduct my home office?**
Possibly, and it depends on exclusive and regular business use. Bring the square footage of both the office and the home and your preparer can run it.
