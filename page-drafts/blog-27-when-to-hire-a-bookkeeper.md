## Quick answer

Hire a bookkeeper when the time you spend on the books is worth more spent on the business, when
you can no longer answer what you made last month, or when your accounts have stopped reconciling.
For most small businesses that arrives somewhere between the first employee and the first hundred
transactions a month.

## Body

### The honest version

Nobody needs a bookkeeper on day one. A sole trader with twenty transactions a month and a
dedicated bank account can keep their own books in an hour, and should.

The question is when that stops being true. It usually stops gradually, and people notice about a
year late.

### Seven signs it is time

**1. You cannot answer what you made last month.** Not roughly — actually. If the answer requires
opening a bank app and adding things up, the books are not doing their job.

**2. The accounts have stopped reconciling.** Once a month goes unreconciled, the next one lands on
top of it. This is the single most reliable early warning, and it is the one people ignore longest.

**3. You have employees or contractors.** Payroll reports, 1099s and W-9s all have to land
correctly in the books, and the deadlines for them come earlier than your return.

**4. Personal and business spending are mixed.** Every month this continues makes the eventual
untangling more expensive, and it weakens the liability separation an LLC exists to provide.

**5. You are doing the books at night or at the weekend.** Value the hours honestly. If you bill
$100 an hour and spend six hours a month on bookkeeping, you are spending $600 to save a few
hundred.

**6. Your tax preparer asks questions you cannot answer.** That is the books failing, one year
late, at the most expensive possible moment.

**7. You are about to apply for a loan or bring in a partner.** Both want financial statements, and
both want them to have existed before the conversation started.

### If you have already waited

Most people arrive here having waited longer than they meant to. That is normal and it is fixable.
The books get caught up first as a separate piece of work, and every cleanup is finished in 14

### What "up to date" actually means

Not "the receipts are in a folder." Up to date means every account reconciled to the statement,
every transaction categorized, and a profit and loss statement and balance sheet you could hand to
a lender this afternoon.

If yours does not meet that description, the books are behind even if nothing feels wrong yet.

## FAQ

**When should a small business hire a bookkeeper?**
Usually when transaction volume passes what you can comfortably reconcile monthly, when you take on
employees or contractors, or when you can no longer answer what you made last month without
adding it up yourself.

**Can I just do it myself with software?**
Early on, yes, and many people should. Software categorizes transactions; it does not know which
categorization is right, and it will not notice that an account stopped reconciling in March.

**How many transactions a month is too many?**
There is no fixed number, but past roughly a hundred a month most owners find the reconciliation
stops fitting into the time they have.

**What if I am already behind?**
Catch it up first, then start monthly. Cleanup runs at $250 an hour after a free assessment, and is
finished in 14 business days or less.
