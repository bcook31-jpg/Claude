## Quick answer (first 40–50 words, straight)

How an LLC files depends on how many members it has. A single-member LLC reports on Schedule C
with your personal return. A multi-member LLC files Form 1065 and issues K-1s. Either way, a
California LLC owes the $800 annual franchise tax whether or not it made money.

---

## Body

### The three things that surprise people

**One. An LLC is not a tax classification.** It is a state legal structure. For federal tax it is
treated as a sole proprietorship, a partnership, or — if you elect it — an S corporation. Which one
you are decides which form you file and when.

**Two. The $800 applies even at a loss.** California charges an annual franchise tax on LLCs
regardless of income. Businesses that made nothing in year one still owe it, and finding that out
in April is the most common unpleasant surprise on a first return.

⚠️ [Verify the current amount, the first-year rules and the due dates at ftb.ca.gov before
publishing. These change and this is the paragraph readers will act on.]

**Three. The business deadline may not be your personal deadline.** A multi-member LLC filing Form
1065 has an earlier deadline than the personal return. Miss it and the penalty accrues per partner
per month, which adds up faster than people expect.

### Which form you file

| Your LLC | Federal form | Filed with |
|---|---|---|
| Single member | Schedule C | Your personal return |
| Multi-member | Form 1065, plus K-1s to each member | Its own return |
| Elected S corp status | Form 1120-S, plus K-1s | Its own return |

Plus the California return and the annual franchise tax filing in every case.

### What you need before you start

- Bank and credit card statements for the full year, for every business account
- Your year-end profit and loss statement and balance sheet
- Formation documents, the EIN letter, and the S corp election if there is one
- Records of anything bought that will be depreciated
- Owner draws, contributions and anything that moved between you and the business
- 1099-NEC and 1099-K forms received
- Any 1099s you issued to contractors, and the W-9s behind them

### The mistakes that cost the most on a first return

- **Personal spending run through the business account.** Very common in year one, and untangling
  it is the most expensive part of the work.
- **No books, just a bank account.** A statement shows what was spent, not what it was for. The
  categorization is the return.
- **Missing estimated tax payments you already made.** They reduce what you owe and people forget
  they made them.
- **Assuming a loss means nothing to file.** The franchise tax and the filing obligation both apply
  anyway.

### Do you need a CPA for LLC taxes?

**No.** An LLC return can be prepared by any preparer holding a valid IRS PTIN who, in California,
is a CPA, an enrolled agent, an attorney, or registered with the California Tax Education Council.

A CPA is required for audited or reviewed financial statements, which is a different thing from
filing a return, and most small LLCs never need one. Where a CPA genuinely helps is a complex
multi-entity structure or a business sale.

What matters more than the letters is whether the person preparing it signs the return, holds a
current credential you can verify, and understands your entity type.

## FAQ

**Do I have to file if my LLC made no money?**
Yes. The filing obligation and the California franchise tax both apply regardless of income.

**When is my LLC return due?**
It depends on how the LLC is treated for tax. A multi-member LLC filing Form 1065 has an earlier
deadline than the personal return. Confirm your dates with your preparer for the year you are
filing. [Deliberately no printed dates — they change, and a stale date costs a client a penalty.]

**Can I file it myself?**
A simple single-member LLC with clean books, yes. It gets harder quickly once there are partners,
assets to depreciate, or an S corp election.

**What is the $800 franchise tax?**
An annual California charge on LLCs that applies whether or not the business made money.
[Verify current amount and first-year rules.]

**Do I need a separate bank account for my LLC?**
Yes, in practice. Mixing personal and business spending makes the return more expensive to prepare
and weakens the liability separation the LLC exists to provide.
