### What is involved (accordion)

⚠️ [Verify each step and every fee against the primary sources before publishing. These change.]

1. **Choose the structure and the name**, and check the name is available with the Secretary of
   State
2. **Incorporate** — file articles of incorporation with California
3. **Get an EIN** from the IRS
4. **Bylaws and the first board meeting**, including conflict-of-interest policy
5. **Apply to the IRS for exemption** — Form 1023 or the shorter 1023-EZ, depending on size and
   eligibility. https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/application-for-recognition-of-exemption
6. **Apply for California exemption** with the Franchise Tax Board
7. **Register with the Attorney General's Registry of Charities and Fundraisers**, which most new
   non-profits do not know exists
8. **Initial Statement of Information**, within 90 days [link page 32]

Steps 6 and 7 are the ones almost everyone misses when they do this themselves, and both carry
consequences later.

## FAQ

**Do I need a lawyer to start a non-profit?**
Not usually. Incorporation and the exemption application are filings, and a firm that does them
regularly can handle both. A lawyer is worth it where the structure is unusual or there is
property, litigation or a complex governance arrangement involved.

**What is the difference between incorporating and getting 501(c)(3) status?**
Incorporation makes the organisation exist under California law. 501(c)(3) is a separate IRS
determination that donations to it are deductible and it does not pay federal income tax. You need
both, and they are applied for separately.
