## ⛔ Read this before drafting further

This is the highest-volume page on the map, and it is the one with a real risk attached.

The keyword is "accountant". The results page is dominated by CPA firms. The buyer typing it
often means a CPA, even when a bookkeeper and tax preparer would serve them better.

Three questions decide whether this page ships and what it says:

1. **Is anyone on staff a CPA?** If yes, this page is straightforward.
2. **Is anyone an enrolled agent?** If yes, the page leads with that. An enrolled agent is a
   federally licensed credential and it is a genuine answer to this search — it is worth saying
   plainly rather than avoiding the credential question.
3. **If neither**, this page still works, but it must be written as *"small business accounting
   services"* — the work — rather than as *"we are accountants"* — the credential. Bookkeeping,
   financial statements and business tax preparation are accurately described as accounting
   services. Claiming or implying a licence nobody holds is not a marketing risk, it is a
   regulatory one. California restricts who may hold out as a CPA.

**I have drafted the version that assumes no CPA on staff**, because that is the safe default
and I do not know the answer. If a CPA or enrolled agent is on staff, tell me and I will rewrite
the hero and the credential section to lead with it, which will convert considerably better.

### The case for one firm doing both (accordion)

When the bookkeeper and the tax preparer are different firms, the return gets prepared from a
file the preparer did not build, questions bounce between two offices in April, and adjustments
made at tax time never make it back into the books. Doing both removes that entirely. The books
are built the way the return needs them from the start.
