## ⚠️ Read this first — the constraint is legal, not editorial

The keyword is "accountant". California regulates that word more tightly than most states.

- **Business and Professions Code section 5058** prohibits titles likely to be confused with
  "certified public accountant" or "public accountant".
  [https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/business-and-professions-code/bpc-sect-5058/]
- The Board of Accountancy went further by regulation and prohibited unlicensed persons from
  using the title "accountant" or describing their services as "accounting" at all.
- **Moore v. California State Board of Accountancy (1992) 2 Cal.4th 999** narrowed that. The
  California Supreme Court held the scheme may constitutionally ban only those uses of
  "accountant" and "accounting" that could mislead the public about licence status — and that
  where the terms appear with a modifier or an express disclaimer dispelling confusion, such as
  a statement that the services offered do not require a state licence, the use may not be
  constitutionally enjoined.
  [https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-supreme-court/1774241.html]

**What that means in practice.** You can describe accounting services with a clear, prominent
disclaimer. You cannot use the word bare, and you certainly cannot use "CPA", "public
accountant" or anything shaped like them.

**I am not your lawyer and this is not legal advice.** I have written two versions below.
Version A carries no risk. Version B chases the 5,400-search term with the disclaimer Moore
describes. **Have a California attorney or the Board of Accountancy confirm before publishing
Version B.** The cost of getting this wrong is a Board complaint, not a ranking drop.

## Who we are (visible — this is the trust hinge, and honesty is the asset)

We are CTEC-registered tax preparers and bookkeepers. We are not a CPA firm.

For most small businesses that distinction costs you nothing and saves you money. California
requires anyone preparing tax returns for a fee to be a CPA, an enrolled agent, an attorney, or
registered with the California Tax Education Council. CTEC registration means 60 hours of
qualifying education, a $5,000 surety bond, a background check, an IRS Preparer Tax
Identification Number, and 20 hours of continuing education renewed every year.
[https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/california-tax-education-council.html]

**Where you do need a CPA, we will tell you.** If a bank wants audited or reviewed financial
statements, only a CPA can issue those. We will say so rather than take the work.

**Our CTEC registration number is A045646.** You can verify it at ctec.org before you call us.

### The case for one firm doing both (accordion)

When the bookkeeper and the tax preparer are different firms, the return gets prepared from a
file the preparer did not build, questions bounce between two offices in April, and adjustments
made at tax time never make it back into the books. Doing both removes that. The books are built
the way the return needs them from the start.

## FAQ

**Are you CPAs?**
No. We are CTEC-registered tax preparers and bookkeepers. California requires anyone preparing
returns for a fee to hold a credential or be registered, and ours is the registered tax preparer
credential. For preparing and filing returns and keeping books, that is the credential the work
requires. If your situation needs audited financial statements or representation before the IRS,
we will tell you and point you to someone who can do it.

**Do I need a CPA for my small business?**
Usually not. Most small businesses need clean books and an accurate return, and neither requires
a CPA licence. You need one for audited or reviewed statements, and for some complex multi-entity
or transaction work. [Link page 18.]
