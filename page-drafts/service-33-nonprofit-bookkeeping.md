## Collapsed sections

### What non-profit bookkeeping actually involves (accordion)

- **Restricted and unrestricted funds tracked separately**, so you can answer a donor or a grantor
  without reconstructing a year
- **Grant tracking**, with spending traceable to the award it came from
- **Programme, administration and fundraising split**, because that split is what your annual
  return and your donors both look at
- **Board-ready monthly reports** — a statement of activities and a statement of financial
  position, not a small-business profit and loss with the words changed
- **In-kind donations recorded** properly rather than left off
- **Year-end close** that the annual return is prepared from

### The annual return (accordion)

Every tax-exempt organisation files annually with the IRS, and which form depends on the size of
the organisation. Losing exempt status for failure to file is automatic after three consecutive
years, and getting it back costs money and time.

**Annual returns from $500.**

https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/annual-filing-and-forms

**Also confirm the California side.** Non-profits generally have Franchise Tax Board obligations
and a registration with the Attorney General's Registry of Charities and Fundraisers alongside the
federal return. If you handle those, say so — it is a real differentiator. If you do not, say that
just as plainly.]

### Why a general bookkeeper struggles with this (accordion)

Most bookkeeping answers one question: did the business make money. Non-profit accounting answers
a different one: was the money spent the way it was promised. Fund restrictions, grant reporting
and the functional expense split have no equivalent in a small-business file, which is why a
non-profit's books so often need rebuilding before a first return can be prepared from them.

If yours are in that state, cleanup comes first. [Link page 1.]
