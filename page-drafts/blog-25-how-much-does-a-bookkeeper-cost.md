## Quick answer

Most small businesses pay $200 to $800 a month for bookkeeping, depending on how many accounts
they have and how many transactions run through them. Hourly rates run roughly $40 to $250. A flat
monthly rate is usually better value than hourly, but only if it has a stated scope.

## Body

### The two ways bookkeepers charge

**Monthly flat rate.** You pay the same figure each month for a defined amount of work. Easier to
budget, and it removes the incentive for the bookkeeper to take longer.

**Hourly.** Common for cleanup, catch-up and irregular work, where nobody can predict the hours
honestly. Rates vary enormously by who is doing the work.

For ongoing monthly bookkeeping, flat is almost always the better arrangement. For fixing a mess,
hourly is the honest one, because a flat quote on unknown work is either padded or about to be
revised.

### What actually drives the price

- **Number of accounts.** Every bank, credit card and loan account has to be reconciled
  separately.
- **Transaction volume.** The single biggest factor. A business running 80 transactions a month is
  a different job from one running 800.
- **Inventory.** Adds real work every month.
- **Payroll.** Whether payroll reports have to be brought in and reconciled.
- **How clean the inputs are.** A business with a dedicated account and no personal spending is
  faster than one where everything is mixed.
- **How fast you answer questions.** Genuinely. A client who answers in a day costs less to serve
  than one who answers in a fortnight.

### The question to ask about any flat rate

**"What happens if I go over?"**

This is where most bookkeeping quotes fall apart. A $199 monthly rate with hourly overages is not
a $199 monthly rate. The advertised price and the invoice disagree from month three, and by then
you have moved your books.

Ask for the scope in writing: how many accounts, how many transactions, and what happens when you
exceed it. A provider who tells you before the change rather than on the invoice is worth paying
more for.

### In-house versus outsourced

A part-time in-house bookkeeper costs more than most people expect once payroll taxes, software,
training and the cost of them leaving are counted. The honest comparison is not the hourly rate
against the monthly fee, it is the fully loaded cost of an employee against the fee.

Outsourcing wins for most businesses under a certain size, and stops winning once the volume
justifies a full-time person. If you are near that line, the number to look at is hours, not
dollars.

## FAQ

**How much does a bookkeeper cost per month?**
Most small businesses pay $200 to $800 a month, driven mainly by number of accounts and monthly
transaction volume.

**What is a typical hourly rate for a bookkeeper?**
Roughly $40 to $250 an hour depending on experience and the type of work. Cleanup and catch-up
work sits at the higher end because it is harder than maintaining clean books.

**Is a flat monthly rate better than hourly?**
For ongoing monthly work, usually yes, provided the scope is stated. For fixing books that are
behind, hourly is more honest.

**Why is bookkeeping cleanup more expensive than monthly bookkeeping?**
Because reconstructing is harder than maintaining. Cleanup means finding what was never entered
and correcting what was entered wrong, often across years.

**Does a bookkeeper also do my taxes?**
Not always — many bookkeepers do not prepare returns, and many preparers do not keep books. We do
both, which is the point of using one firm.
