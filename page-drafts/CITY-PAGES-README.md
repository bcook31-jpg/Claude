# Read this before building any city page (pages 8 to 15)

Eight of the nineteen deadline pages are the same service in a different city. That is the exact
shape Google's scaled-content policy targets, so how they are built decides whether they get
indexed at all.

**The evidence, from `references/doorway-pages.md`:** near-duplicate city pages mostly do not get
penalized — they get ignored. One documented programmatic city site with 33,620 templated pages
had roughly 18% indexed. The same site hand-wrote 80 to 120 words of genuinely region-specific
content for its top 15 states and indexation rose 15% in six weeks. The failure mode is silence,
not a penalty.

**The rule Google's own guidance points at,** and the best single line on the subject, from John
Mueller in 2018: if you have nothing to add to a page other than a city name, fold the pages
together and make one strong page instead.

## So the test each of these must pass before it is built

Each city page needs **at least three** of the following, and all three must be real:

1. A named client or job in that city, with detail, used with permission
2. A review from a client in that city, word for word
3. Something true about doing this work in that city specifically — the industries that dominate
   it, a local filing quirk, the languages the office serves there
4. A city-specific FAQ answer that would be wrong for the neighbouring city
5. A genuine reason a business there chooses you over the firms already in that city

**Fewer than three: do not build the page.** Fold that city into the "Areas we serve" section of
the hub (page 4 or 7) as a paragraph and a link to the contact page. One strong hub beats seven
thin spokes, and it costs nothing in rankings you were going to get anyway.

## What I have and have not done in these drafts

Each draft below carries a **suggested angle** — a starting hypothesis about what makes that
city's page different. Those angles are hypotheses drawn from general knowledge of the area, not
verified facts about your client base, and **none of them should be published as written**.
Confirm or replace each one from what you actually know about working in that city.

I have deliberately not written filler paragraphs about each city's history or character. That
material is what makes a page look unique to a human and still reads as templated to Google,
because every competitor generates the same thing. Real client work is the only durable
differentiator here.

## Recommended order if the Hibu page budget is small

If you can only publish a handful of pages, city pages are not where they go. The order by
expected return is:

1. Page 1 — Bookkeeping cleanup (difficulty 16, national demand, highest cost-per-click)
2. Page 4 — Bookkeeping services (recurring revenue, flat year-round demand)
3. Page 7 — Tax preparation, Lakewood (fixes the homepage's scattered city rankings)
4. Page 6 — Small business tax preparation (sharpest seasonal deadline)

Page 8 (Long Beach) is the only city page with measurable search volume and a real difficulty
score. Every other city page is speculative until the hub pages are working.
