## Quick answer

A bookkeeper records what happened. A tax preparer files your return. A CPA is a state-licensed
credential that adds audited financial statements and unlimited IRS representation. Most small
businesses need the first two and never need the third.

## Body

### What each one actually does

**A bookkeeper** keeps the records. Reconciles accounts, categorizes transactions, produces monthly
financial statements. No licence is required in California, which surprises people — the skill is
real but the title is unregulated.

**A tax preparer** prepares and files returns. In California anyone doing this for a fee must be a
CPA, an enrolled agent, an attorney, or registered with the California Tax Education Council, which
requires 60 hours of qualifying education, a $5,000 surety bond, a background check and annual
renewal.

**A CPA** is a state-licensed accounting credential. Degree requirements, four exam parts,
qualifying experience and ongoing education, licensed by the California Board of Accountancy. CPAs
can do everything above and two things nobody else can: issue audited or reviewed financial
statements, and represent any taxpayer before any IRS office without limitation.

### The overlap is where the confusion lives

Plenty of bookkeepers do not prepare returns. Plenty of tax preparers do not keep books. And a CPA
firm may do both, or may only do the return and hand the bookkeeping to someone else.

So the useful question is not which title you need. It is which of these three jobs you need done,
and whether one firm does all of them.

### When you genuinely need a CPA

- A bank, investor or landlord has asked for **audited or reviewed financial statements**. Only a
  CPA can issue these. There is no substitute.
- You are **selling the business**, or restructuring across multiple entities.
- Something is **already in dispute** with the IRS at appeals or collections, where unlimited
  representation rights matter.

### When you do not

- A W-2 return, even with a mortgage, children and investments
- A sole proprietorship or single-member LLC with clean books
- A straightforward S corporation or partnership return
- Ordinary monthly bookkeeping
- Rental property, unless the structure is unusual

That covers most small businesses in Lakewood, and paying CPA rates for it is money spent on
training you are not using.

### The credential nobody has heard of

There is a fourth option between a registered preparer and a CPA: the **IRS Annual Filing Season
Program**. Preparers who complete it earn a Record of Completion, are listed in the IRS public
directory alongside CPAs and enrolled agents, and gain **limited representation rights** — they can
represent you before IRS revenue agents, customer service and the Taxpayer Advocate Service, but
only on a return they personally prepared and signed, and not before appeals or collections.

*Brian Cook holds one. That is why we can help with a notice on a return we prepared, and why we
will tell you plainly when a matter needs someone with unlimited rights.*

### How to check anyone in two minutes

- **CPA:** the California Board of Accountancy licence lookup
- **CPA, enrolled agent or Annual Filing Season Program participant:** the IRS directory of
  preparers [https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/faqs-directory-of-federal-tax-return-preparers-with-credentials-and-select-qualifications]
- **CTEC registered:** ctec.org

Do this before handing anyone your documents. It takes less time than finding your W-2.

### The practical answer for most small businesses

You need someone keeping the books monthly and someone filing the return, and there is a real
advantage in those being the same firm. When they are different, the return gets prepared from a
file the preparer did not build, questions bounce between two offices in April, and adjustments
made at tax time never make it back into the books.

## FAQ

**Do I need a CPA or a bookkeeper?**
Most small businesses need a bookkeeper for the monthly work and a qualified preparer for the
return. A CPA is required for audited or reviewed financial statements and for representation
before IRS appeals or collections.

**Can a bookkeeper file my taxes?**
Only if they also hold a preparer credential. In California, preparing returns for a fee requires
CPA, enrolled agent, attorney or CTEC registration. Many bookkeepers hold none of these.

**Is a bookkeeper cheaper than a CPA?**
Yes, usually substantially. The work is different and so is the training behind it.

**Do I need a licence to be a bookkeeper in California?**
No. Bookkeeping is unregulated in California. Preparing tax returns for a fee is not.

**Can one firm do both?**
Yes, and it is usually the better arrangement, because the books get built the way the return
needs them.
