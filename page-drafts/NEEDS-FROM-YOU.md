# What the 19 drafts need before any of them can be published

Every draft is structurally complete. None of them can go to Hibu as written, because a money
page without a price, a proof number or a credential does not convert, and I will not invent any
of the three.

This list is ordered by how many pages each item unblocks.

---

## 1. ✅ RESOLVED — the credential answer

**Confirmed 2026-09-07: CTEC-registered tax preparers. No CPA on staff.**

All affected pages are rewritten. What changed:

- **Page 5** was "Small business accounting" and is now "Small business bookkeeping and tax
  services". California restricts unlicensed use of "accountant" and "accounting" — B&P Code
  5058, narrowed by *Moore v. California State Bd. of Accountancy* (1992) 2 Cal.4th 999, which
  permits the terms only alongside a disclaimer that dispels confusion about licence status. The
  draft now carries two versions: a safe one to publish now, and a keyword-forward one that needs
  a lawyer's sign-off first. **Publish the safe one.**
- **Schema types changed** on pages 1, 5 and 7, from `AccountingService` to `ProfessionalService`
  and `TaxPreparationService`. Schema is a public machine-readable claim about what the business
  is, so the same restriction applies there.
- **`cpa for llc taxes` was removed** as a secondary from page 6 and moved to blog 24. A service
  page ranking for it implies the service is CPA-provided.
- **Blogs 17, 18 and 19 now state the credential plainly** and convert on it rather than around
  it. Blog 19 in particular is now your strongest article, because every competitor ranking for
  "how much do tax preparers charge" is describing you from the outside.

**✅ CTEC number confirmed 2026-09-07: A045646.** Now in place on pages 5, 17, 18 and 19.

**Still needed:** the author's name for the blog author boxes, and how long you have been
registered. "Registered since [year]" beside the number is stronger than the number alone.

**Still open, and it is a compliance question rather than a marketing one:** several tax-education
providers state the CTEC number must appear in any advertising you run, which would make your
website mandatory rather than optional. I could not verify that against the statute. Ask CTEC
directly. Separately, confirm your intake process delivers the written disclosure that Business
and Professions Code section 22252 requires before services are rendered — name, address,
telephone, evidence of bond compliance, and CTEC's website address.

**Closed 2026-09-07: no enrolled agent either. CTEC-registered tax preparers, full stop.**
Everything is written for that and nothing further needs changing on credential grounds.

**One consequence worth knowing.** "Bookkeeper" and "bookkeeping" are not restricted terms in
California — no licence or registration is required to use them, and CTEC registration governs
paid tax return preparation, not bookkeeping. That means the entire bookkeeping side of the map,
pages 1, 2, 3, 4, 20, 21, 22, 25, 26 and 27, uses vocabulary you can claim without qualification
or disclaimer. Page 5 was the only page the credential constraint ever touched.

That is a better position than it sounds. Your four highest-priority pages are all bookkeeping,
they are the lowest difficulty on the map, they carry the highest cost-per-click, and none of
them needs a legal read.

## 2. Pricing — blocks 9 pages

A starting price and what moves it, for each of:

- Monthly bookkeeping (page 4)
- Bookkeeping cleanup, per year of books (page 1)
- QuickBooks cleanup or file review (page 2)
- Individual tax return (page 7)
- Business return, by entity type (page 6)

Every page competing with you on these terms publishes a price. A page without one loses to a
page with a higher one.

## 3. Turnaround times — blocks 3 pages

Real, honest, not best-case. How long for a year of books (pages 1 and 2), and the fastest you
can realistically move for someone in a panic (page 3). A national competitor advertises 30 days
for a year of books. If yours is faster, that is a headline. If it is slower, the page still
needs a number.

## 4. Yes or no on each of these services — blocks 6 pages

I have written around all of these rather than assume. Each one changes page copy:

- [ ] Do you prepare prior-year and late returns?
- [ ] Do you file extensions?
- [ ] Do you prepare multi-state returns?
- [ ] Do you file S corporation and partnership returns?
- [ ] QuickBooks Online only, or Desktop as well?
- [ ] Is anyone a certified QuickBooks ProAdvisor?

## 5. Reviews — improves every page

Two or three real client reviews, word for word, with permission. Ideally one that mentions being
behind on books and one that mentions a business return. Real customer language outperforms
anything I can write.

## 6. Operational details — blocks 2 pages

- Phone number as it should appear
- Real office hours, including any extended tax-season hours
- Appointment, walk-in and drop-off policy
- Languages spoken in the office — a genuine differentiator in this service area and worth stating
- Whether you are open year-round
- How clients send documents: portal, email or drop-off
- Your policy if the IRS sends a notice after filing

## 7. Photos — improves every page

- The office, exterior and interior (page 7 especially — it is your location signal)
- Real photos of the people clients meet
- A before-and-after reconciliation or chart-of-accounts screen, client detail removed. This is
  the most persuasive single image available for pages 1 and 2 and no competitor has one.

**No stock faces anywhere.** Every draft says this and it matters more on a page about money.

## 8. The checklist PDF — blocks page 16

Page 16 targets "small business tax deductions checklist pdf" among others. That secondary is a
request for a file. The article is written; the file needs building. One page, tick boxes, your
logo and phone number. It is also the document you send every business client before their
appointment, so it saves your team time in the weeks you have none.

## 9. Local material — blocks 8 pages

Pages 8 through 15 are the city pages, and they are gated deliberately. Each needs three real
items from `CITY-PAGES-README.md`: a named local client or job, a local review, something true
about working in that city, a city-specific FAQ answer, or a real reason a business there chooses
you.

**Fewer than three for a given city: do not build that page.** Add the city to the hub's "areas
we serve" section instead. That is the better outcome. Seven thin city pages mostly do not get
indexed, and while they sit there they drag the quality signal across the whole site.

## Three decisions only you can make

**The Hibu page budget.** Hibu's published terms cap managed page builds at four new pages per
service year on most packages, with five or ten more available as a paid add-on. Smart Sites
customers can add pages themselves. Nineteen pages assumes the second situation. If you are on the
first, the plan is pages 1, 4, 7 and 6, in that order, and the rest waits.

**Pages 1, 2 and 3 overlap.** They target one buyer through three doors: the service, the
software, and the panic. Read them side by side before building. If two say the same thing in the
same order, merge them and keep the losing page's keywords as secondaries on the survivor. That
judgment needs your eyes on your own market.

**Two legal checks now.** Page 5 Version B uses "accounting services" with a disclaimer modelled
on *Moore*. Do not publish it without a California attorney or the Board of Accountancy
confirming the wording. Version A needs no review and should go up now.

Page 19 states what a preparer may and may not charge, including that
contingent fees are generally prohibited for ordinary return preparation under Circular 230. I
have written it carefully and linked primary sources, but it is the only page in this batch making
claims about what is legally permitted. Have someone confirm those sentences, or soften them and
lean harder on the links.
