## ⛔ Three places the drafts were wrong

### 1. Bookkeeping is not $250 a month. Your two real clients pay $350 and $750.

Every bookkeeping line in twenty months of invoices:

```
$750  Bookkeeping - Monthly        $350  Bookkeeping - Monthly
$750  Bookkeeping - April 2026     $350  Bookkeeping - May 2026
$750  Bookkeeping - May 2026       $350  Bookkeeping - June 2026
$750  Bookkeeping - June 2026
```

$250 would be **below the floor you have ever charged**, and roughly a third of what your larger
client pays. Publishing it would anchor every new enquiry a third under your real price and it
would sit badly with the two clients already paying more.

**The tiers should start at $350.** Suggested, matched to what you actually bill:

| Tier | Price | Accounts | Transactions/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essentials | $350 | Up to 2 | Up to 100 |
| Standard | $550 | Up to 4 | Up to 250 |
| Full | $750 | Up to 6 | Up to 450 |

Your two live clients land at the top and bottom of that ladder, which is a good sign the shape is
right. Note this also removes the earlier worry about the entry tier being one hour of work —
$350 buys about ninety minutes at your rate, which fits 100 transactions far more comfortably.

### 2. A personal return is $295 typically, not $250.

163 invoiced returns, median $295, and $250 is near the bottom of the range. "From $250" is
defensible but it under-sells you by 15% on your highest-volume service. **"Most personal returns
are $295"** is both true and stronger, because a specific number reads as a real price while a
"from" reads as a teaser.

### 3. $950 for a business return is only right for corporations.

Corporate and S corporation work does run around $900. But a personal return with a Schedule C or
an LLC lands at **$422 median**, less than half that. Page 6 currently leads with $950, which will
frighten off every sole proprietor and single-member LLC — and those are 32 of your invoices
against 27 corporate.

**Split it on the page:**

- Personal return with self-employment or a single-member LLC — from $445
- Partnership or multi-member LLC — [needs your figure]
- S corporation or C corporation — from $950

## Two things the keyword map missed entirely

**Non-profit returns.** Six invoices at $500, plus one non-profit formation at $750. That is a
real service line with real revenue and it is nowhere on the map. Non-profit and Form 990 keywords
are typically low competition because most local firms do not touch them. Worth a keyword pull.

**Statement of Information filings.** $50 to $100, and blog post 30 is already about California
LLC annual costs including exactly this filing. That post can route straight into a service you
already sell.

## The strategic finding, stated plainly

**Bookkeeping is 7 line items out of 200. Tax preparation is roughly 82% of your invoices.**

The keyword map bets heavily on bookkeeping — 11 of 31 pages, and the top four in the build order.
**I still think that bet is right**, because bookkeeping is recurring, it holds flat demand through
your off-season, it carries the lowest difficulty scores on the map, and the cleanup cluster has
the highest cost-per-click in the whole keyword set.

But it should be called what it is: **you are building a new service line, not scaling an existing
one.** Two clients is not a book of business. That changes what proof you can put on those pages —
you cannot claim depth of bookkeeping experience you do not yet have — and it means the tax pages
carry the site's credibility while bookkeeping grows behind them.

The $2,000 cleanup invoice is the exception and it is encouraging. One job at $2,000 is worth
about seven personal returns.
